**[H3: SLA vs DLP vs LCD Technologies]** (500-550 words)

**[TECHNOLOGY COMPARISON INTRO]** (Medium paragraph)
Stereolithography (SLA), Digital Light Processing (DLP), and LCD printing technologies each offer distinct advantages for different dental applications, with selection depending on priorities for speed, precision, surface quality, and production volume. Understanding these fundamental differences guides equipment selection toward systems that optimize your intended applications rather than simply choosing the latest or most marketed technology.

**[COMPARISON TABLE: Technology Specifications]**
| Technology | Layer Resolution | Print Speed | Surface Quality | Best Applications |
|------------|------------------|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **SLA** | 25-100 microns | Medium (15-25mm/hr) | Excellent | Surgical guides, detailed work |
| **DLP** | 35-100 microns | Fast (25-50mm/hr) | Very good | High-volume production |
| **LCD** | 35-75 microns | Very fast (30-60mm/hr) | Good | Cost-effective volume work |

**[SLA TECHNOLOGY DEEP DIVE]** (Long paragraph - 6 sentences)
Stereolithography represents the gold standard for dental applications requiring exceptional surface quality and fine detail reproduction, using precise laser control to cure liquid resin with unmatched accuracy. SLA systems excel at capturing margin lines, contact points, and surface textures essential for restorative work, making them ideal for surgical guides, temporary crowns, and precision applications. The technology's slower printing speed is offset by superior surface quality that often eliminates post-processing requirements, reducing total production time for complex geometries. Material compatibility tends to be broader with SLA systems, offering access to specialized dental resins for diverse applications from rigid surgical guides to flexible night guards. Leading SLA manufacturers include Formlabs, 3D Systems, and Asiga, each offering dental-specific systems with validated workflows and comprehensive support programs. While SLA equipment typically costs more initially, the combination of precision, surface quality, and material options often justifies the investment for practices prioritizing clinical excellence.
